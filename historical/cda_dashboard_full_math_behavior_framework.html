Purpose, economic framework, and methodology

1. What this experiment is testing

This dashboard simulates a corrected Continuous Double Auction (CDA) and tests whether the realized market price path converges toward the competitive equilibrium implied by the chosen supply and demand curves. The market is not centrally cleared. Prices emerge from bilateral sequential trades through an order book.

The behavioral aggressiveness/frustration state s_i is a novel construction added to mimic human impatience, adaptation, and changing willingness to trade after failed attempts.

Demand: P_D(Q) = a − bQ
Supply: P_S(Q) = c + dQ
Q* = (a − c) / (b + d)
P* = c + dQ*

The main question is: when agents trade through a decentralized CDA institution, do realized prices cluster near P*, and does the aggregate convergence resemble a simple exponential approach?

2. Economic environment

The simulation creates N buyers and N sellers. Buyer values and seller costs come directly from the chosen supply and demand curves:

v_i = a − b(i + 0.5)
cost_j = c + d(j + 0.5)

Buyer and seller surplus are:

Buyer surplus = v_i − p
Seller surplus = p − cost_j

The dashboard checks for negative-surplus violations. A clean CDA run should not force buyers to pay above value or sellers to sell below cost.

3. Trader state variables and novel behavioral construction

Novel behavioral layer

The CDA institution is standard, but this dashboard adds a novel behavioral layer to mimic human-like frustration, impatience, and aggressiveness. Each active trader carries an internal state variable:

s_i ≥ 1

s_i is a frustration/aggressiveness parameter. A trader with higher s_i is more willing to improve quotes and more likely to accept a favorable standing quote.

Buyer value vᵢ
Maximum price buyer i can rationally pay.
Seller cost cⱼ
Minimum price seller j can rationally accept.
Buyer state sᵇᵢ
Buyer frustration/aggressiveness multiplier.
Seller state sˢⱼ
Seller frustration/aggressiveness multiplier.

This is not a textbook CDA assumption. It is a deliberate modeling choice to represent how human traders may become more impatient after not getting a trade.

4. Frustration/aggressiveness update rule

Human-behavior proxy

In the implemented simulation, s_i increases when a trader attempts to act but cannot improve the current best quote. That condition represents an unsuccessful attempt to move the market.

s_i ← min(s_max, s_i · 1.5^(1/5))

In the dashboard code:

s_max = 4
step multiplier = 1.5^(1/5)

The cap keeps behavior bounded. The idea is not to make traders irrational; it is to make their quote-improvement intensity adapt after friction.

Earlier versions of the model explored a rejection-counter form: s(n+1)=s(n)·1.5^(k/5), where k is a failed-attempt count. The dashboard uses the simpler event-based form above: a failed quote-improvement attempt multiplies s_i by 1.5^(1/5).

5. Quote-posting rules

If there is no standing quote, agents begin from initialized proportional quotes:

Initial buyer bid: B_i = α_b v_i
Initial seller ask: A_j = α_s cost_j

Once the book exists, buyers improve the best bid upward and sellers improve the best ask downward. The behavioral state s_i controls the step size:

new bid = best bid + δ₀sᵇᵢ
new ask = best ask − δ₀sˢⱼ

Rationality constraints are enforced:

bid ≤ v_i
ask ≥ cost_j

6. Acceptance probability functions

The probability functions use only the current quote, private value/cost, and the trader's internal aggressiveness state s_i. They do not use P*.

Buyer accepts best ask A* if A* ≤ vᵢ:

Pr(buyer accepts A*) = min(1, sᵇᵢ · max(0, 1 − A*/v_i))

Seller accepts best bid B* if B* ≥ costⱼ:

Pr(seller accepts B*) = min(1, sˢⱼ · max(0, 1 − cost_j/B*))

These functions are bounded in [0,1]. Higher s_i makes a trader more likely to accept a profitable standing quote.

7. CDA institution and trade execution

  • A random active buyer or seller is selected each event.
  • A buyer may accept the best ask or post/improve a bid.
  • A seller may accept the best bid or post/improve an ask.
  • If a buyer's bid crosses the best ask, trade executes at the standing ask.
  • If a seller's ask crosses the best bid, trade executes at the standing bid.
  • After a trade, the matched buyer and seller leave the market.

The trade occurs at an actual standing quote. It is not a Walrasian clearing price and not an implied theoretical price.

8. No P* leakage

The simulation is designed so P* does not leak into agent behavior. Traders do not see P*, Q*, β, the ODE curve, residuals, or future prices.

Agents use: private value/cost, current best bid, current best ask, quote tick δ₀, random activation, and their own behavioral state s_i.

Agents do not use: P*, Q*, fitted β, the ODE curve, future prices, residual diagnostics, or the aggregate supply/demand solution.

Even s_i updates only from local trading conditions. It is not nudged toward P*. That makes convergence a genuine emergent result of the CDA mechanism plus adaptive behavior.

9. Why this is economically legitimate

A CDA is a decentralized market institution: prices are produced by sequential buyer-seller interactions, not by a central auctioneer. P* is still useful as a competitive benchmark because it is where aggregate supply and demand balance.

  • Market mechanism: decentralized CDA trading.
  • Agent information: local private and order-book information only.
  • Behavioral layer: novel bounded frustration/aggressiveness state s_i.
  • Evaluation benchmark: P* and Q* computed after the fact.

10. Order-book formation

The early part of a CDA run is book formation. The dashboard detects formation using only the live spread:

Spread_t = BestAsk_t − BestBid_t

Once the rolling median spread falls below the chosen threshold and the burn-in condition is satisfied, the dashboard treats the order book as formed.

This formation rule avoids P* leakage because it uses only observable book state.

11. ODE convergence envelope

After book formation, the dashboard fits a one-parameter exponential convergence envelope to the moving-average price path:

P(k) = P* + (P₀ − P*) exp(−(k − k₀)/β)

β is the only free parameter. The fit asks whether CDA prices broadly behave like a drag-style convergence process, not whether every individual contract price is exactly exponential.

12. Why R² may be low or negative

R² is expected to be low, unstable, or even negative in some runs. The CDA path is stochastic and sequential, while the ODE fit has only one free parameter, β.

If the market is already near equilibrium after formation, there may be little directional variation left for the exponential curve to explain.

13. Residual analysis

The residual is:

e(k) = P_obs,MA(k) − P_ODE(k)
  • MSE: average squared residual.
  • RMSE: residual size in price units.
  • MAE: average absolute residual.
  • Signed integral: ∫e(k)dk, where positive and negative regions cancel.
  • Absolute area: ∫|e(k)|dk, the total residual magnitude.

If signed residuals roughly cancel relative to absolute area, the residuals look more like balanced practical noise around the envelope rather than a one-sided structural miss.

14. What matters most

  • Final P* deviation: do realized prices cluster near competitive equilibrium?
  • β: how quickly does the convergence envelope approach P*?
  • MSE/RMSE: how large are deviations in price units?
  • Signed integral / absolute area: are residuals balanced or biased?
  • Monte Carlo stability: do results survive repeated random runs?

15. Why AC(1) / AR(1) is not used here

Tick-to-tick autocorrelation is not the right main metric because CDA prices are sequential, discrete, and path-dependent by construction. A high short-run autocorrelation can simply reflect market microstructure persistence rather than failure of convergence.
